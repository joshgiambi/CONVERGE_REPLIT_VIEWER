A few things to double-check now that you’ve switched to a true DICOM-based projection:
	1.	Make sure you’ve pulled the right DICOM tags
	•	In your code you call primaryImage.imageOrientation but the standard tag is ImageOrientationPatient (x00200037)—that’s the 6-element array [rX, rY, rZ, cX, cY, cZ].
	•	Likewise PixelSpacing is x00280030 (two values: rowSpacing, colSpacing). If any of those come back as strings, split on “\” and Number(…) them.
	2.	Swap your dot products
We want

// how much world-mm shift moves you along each image axis:
pxOffsetX = dot(delta, rowCosine) / colSpacing;   // moving along world vector projected onto rows changes your **column** index
pxOffsetY = dot(delta, colCosine) / rowSpacing;   // world → columns changes your **row** index

But you did the opposite—i.e. you divided rowCosine by rowSpacing.  Try swapping those two lines and see if it flips your lateral vs. vertical offset.

	3.	Log your cosines & signs
Before you draw, add:

console.log({
  rowCosine,
  colCosine,
  rowSpacing,
  colSpacing,
  delta,
  pxOffsetX,
  pxOffsetY
});

and verify that:
	•	rowCosine points roughly left→right in world space,
	•	colCosine points roughly top→bottom,
	•	a pure shift to the patient’s right yields a positive pxOffsetX.

	4.	Check for a sign flip on Y
Canvas Y grows down but DICOM Y grows up.  You’re doing drawY = baseY - dy_px, which is correct if dy_px is DICOM-upwards.  If you still see a vertical offset, try + dy_px instead—sometimes the IOP vectors are negated.
	5.	Verify your registration matrix
If your 4×4 matrix contains any rotation or scaling in the top-left 2×2 block, you can’t treat it as a pure translation + orientation.  In that case you must either:

ctx.setTransform(
  a*scaleX,  c*scaleY,
  b*scaleX,  d*scaleY,
  drawX,     drawY
);
ctx.drawImage(temp, 0, 0);

where a,b,c,d = your matrix[0,1,4,5] entries.

TL;DR:
	1.	Swap how you divide your dot-products:

dx_px = dot(delta, rowCosine) / colSpacing;
dy_px = dot(delta, colCosine) / rowSpacing;


	2.	Double-check your DICOM tags (ImageOrientationPatient, PixelSpacing).
	3.	Log everything (cosines, spacing, delta, px offsets) to confirm direction and sign.

Once those three are in sync, your MRI overlay will land exactly where the CT anatomy is.
