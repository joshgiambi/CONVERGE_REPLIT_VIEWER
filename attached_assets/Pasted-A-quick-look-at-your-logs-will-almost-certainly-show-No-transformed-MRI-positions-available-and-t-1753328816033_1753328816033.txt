A quick look at your logs will almost certainly show:

No transformed MRI positions available

and then the overlay never gets drawn, because in your renderFusionOverlayNew you’ve got:

if (!transformedMRIPositions.current || transformedMRIPositions.current.length === 0) {
  console.log("No transformed MRI positions available");
  return;
}

That guard will fire anytime you haven’t filled transformedMRIPositions.current with anything—so even though you’ve loaded secondaryImages and your registrationMatrix is set, you never actually call

computeTransformedMRIPositions(secondaryImages, registrationMatrix)
  .then(transformed => transformedMRIPositions.current = transformed);

instead, in your secondary‐load effect you do:

console.log("=== FORCING FRESH MRI TRANSFORMATION COMPUTATION ===");
precomputeMRITransformations(sortedImages, registrationMatrix);

but precomputeMRITransformations is nowhere imported or defined—you meant to call computeTransformedMRIPositions. Because of that typo your transformedMRIPositions array remains empty, so your overlay routine always bails out before drawing.

⸻

What you need to do:
	1.	Replace that bogus precomputeMRITransformations(...) call with the real util and actually stash its result in your ref:

useEffect(() => {
  if (registrationMatrix && secondaryImages.length > 0) {
    const transformed = computeTransformedMRIPositions(secondaryImages, registrationMatrix);
    transformedMRIPositions.current = transformed;
    // now you can optionally force a redraw
    displayCurrentImage();
  }
}, [registrationMatrix, secondaryImages]);


	2.	Remove the early‐exit guard (or at least log it less aggressively) once you know transforms are being computed:

if (transformedMRIPositions.current.length === 0) {
  // only warn once, or better yet show a little spinner
  return;
}


	3.	Verify in your console that:

✓ Fusion overlay rendered: CT=…mm, opacity=…, MRI slices=…

actually appears. If you still don’t see MRI on the CT canvas, double-check that:
	•	secondaryImageCache actually contains the slice you expect (inspect actualCache.get(…) inside your util)
	•	Your util’s drawing code (renderFusionOverlayUtil) is using the same canvas size / pan / scale that you passed in.

Once your transformedMRIPositions ref is properly populated, the guard won’t trip and your fused MRI slice should paint on top of the CT image.
